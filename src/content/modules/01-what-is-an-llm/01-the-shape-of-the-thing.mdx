A large language model, viewed from the outside, is a function. You hand it
some text. It hands you back more text. Everything else — the chat UI, the
"thinking", the tools, the personality — is scaffolding around that one
operation.

What makes it interesting is not the function itself but what's inside. A
few hundred billion numbers, arranged in a particular shape, multiplied
together in a particular order. None of those numbers know they're
representing language. The model doesn't know it's a model. The fact that
useful text comes out the other end is, depending on your mood, either a
deep statement about the structure of language or an enormous coincidence
that we have learned how to engineer reliably.

This module is the top of the stack. Over the next eleven we'll work
downward — from this opaque pile of weights, through transformers and GPUs
and instruction sets and logic gates, all the way to the electrons doing
the actual work. By the bottom you should be able to look back up at this
lesson and see, with no magic remaining, why a pattern of charges in
silicon can talk back.
